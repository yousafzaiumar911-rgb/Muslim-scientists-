Ibn al-Haytham

c. 965 – c. 1040

Full Name: Abu Ali al-Hasan ibn al-Haytham

Field: Optics, Physics, Mathematics and Astronomy

Famous Work: Kitab al-Manazir — Book of Optics

Who was Ibn al-Haytham?

Ibn al-Haytham was a major scholar of the Islamic Golden Age. He was born around 965 in Basra and later worked in Egypt. He made important contributions to mathematics, physics, astronomy and especially the study of light and vision.

His Work on Optics

Ibn al-Haytham carried out systematic investigations into light, reflection, refraction and vision. His work challenged earlier ideas about how humans see and emphasized observation and experimentation.

Book of Optics

His most famous work is known as Kitab al-Manazir, or the Book of Optics. It was a major work on light, vision and optical phenomena and was later translated into Latin.

Camera Obscura

Ibn al-Haytham studied the behavior of light entering a dark chamber through a small opening. His work on this phenomenon is closely associated with the historical development of the camera obscura.

Scientific Investigation

A notable feature of his work was the use of observation, experimentation and mathematical reasoning. His approach became an important part of the history of experimental science.

His Legacy

Ibn al-Haytham's writings on optics were translated into Latin and influenced later scholars in Europe. His work remains an important part of the history of optics and the study of vision.
