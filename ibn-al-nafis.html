Who Was Ibn al-Nafis?

Ala al-Din Ibn al-Nafis was a physician and scholar of the 13th-century Islamic world. He received his medical education in Damascus and later moved to Cairo, where he worked at major hospitals.

He wrote extensively on medicine and also studied fields including philosophy, law, theology and astronomy.

❤️ Pulmonary Circulation

Ibn al-Nafis is particularly known for his description of the movement of blood between the heart and lungs.

He rejected the idea that blood could pass directly through invisible pores in the wall separating the right and left ventricles of the heart.

Instead, he described blood traveling from the right side of the heart to the lungs and then returning to the left side of the heart.

📖 Commentary on Avicenna's Canon

One of Ibn al-Nafis's most important works was his Commentary on Anatomy in Avicenna's Canon.

In this work he discussed the anatomy of the heart and lungs and presented his ideas about pulmonary circulation.

🫀 The Heart and Lungs

Ibn al-Nafis explained that the two ventricles of the heart were not connected by passages through the interventricular septum.

His description directed attention toward the lungs as an essential part of the pathway between the right and left sides of the heart.

⚕️ Medical Contributions

Ibn al-Nafis wrote about many areas of medicine. His medical works included discussions of anatomy, physiology, diseases, medicines and medical treatment.

He also produced commentaries on earlier medical authorities, including Hippocrates and Ibn Sina.

📚 Major Works

  • Commentary on Anatomy in Avicenna's Canon
  • Commentary on the Canon of Medicine
  • The Comprehensive Book of Medicine
  • Book of Selection of Nutrients
  • Works on ophthalmology and medicine

🌍 Rediscovery of His Work

His description of pulmonary circulation was not widely known in the modern West for centuries.

In the early 20th century, a manuscript of his Commentary on Anatomy was identified in Berlin by the Egyptian physician Muhyo Al-Deen el-Tatawi. The discovery helped bring Ibn al-Nafis's contribution to wider attention. 1

🌟 His Legacy

Ibn al-Nafis is an important figure in the history of medicine because his anatomical description challenged established Galenic ideas about the heart.

Modern medical-history literature recognizes his work as an important early description of pulmonary circulation. 2
